Reconceptualizing Nursing Ethics in Digital Health: A Relational Systems Model — A Conceptual Study

Adelaide Edina Almeida da Costa; Abdul Wakhid

Journal: International Journal of Health and Social Sciences (IJHSS)

Publisher: Lusofonia Press

Volume: 1

Issue: 1

Publication date: 23 November 2026

Article type: Conceptual Study

Peer review: Independent Peer Review

First page: 1

DOI: https://doi.org/10.68609/reconceptualizing-nursing-ethics-digital-health-2026

Abstract

Introduction: Digital transformation through artificial intelligence (AI), telehealth, electronic health records, remote monitoring, and algorithmic systems is reshaping nursing practice and ethical responsibility. Existing approaches often address professional ethics, technology governance, or sociocultural context separately. This conceptual study addresses that fragmentation by integrating these perspectives.

Methods: A conceptual philosophical–hermeneutic design was combined with an integrative literature synthesis. The search covered literature published from 2015 to 2026 across PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, and Google Scholar, supplemented by reference-list screening and iterative citation tracking. The final conceptual synthesis comprised 25 sources, including recent peer-reviewed journal literature, foundational works, and international frameworks. Because the original project file did not preserve exact database-level retrieval dates or screening counts, these historical details could not be reconstructed without speculation. Concepts were extracted into an analytic matrix, thematically compared across traditions, interpreted hermeneutically, and iteratively organized into model dimensions, layers, outcomes, and propositions.

Results: Three interdependent dimensions were synthesized: relational ethics, socio-technical integration, and sociocultural context. These dimensions operate through five analytically distinct layers—Person, Relational Ethics, Socio-Technical System, Organizational Governance, and Societal Sustainability—and are associated with four proposed outcomes: ethical decision-making, professional autonomy, relational accountability, and workforce adaptability and sustainability.

Conclusions: The Relational Systems Model integrates moral, technological, organizational, and sociocultural perspectives into one conceptual architecture. Its contribution is theoretical rather than empirically established: the model offers propositions for future testing and should not yet be treated as a validated framework for governance or workforce development.

Keywords

Accountability; artificial intelligence; autonomy; sociocultural context; socio-technical systems; workforce adaptability
